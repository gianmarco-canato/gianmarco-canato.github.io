Doctoral research · artistic research

Improvisation through the lens of experimental music theatre

My doctoral research asks how approaching my own improvisational practice through concepts from experimental music theatre shapes the way I act through the means each performance makes available, depending on its dramaturgical framework.

The project

I start from two means of action, bassoon and live electronics, to which text, images and movement are added from one performance to another. Each dramaturgical framework makes these means available in a different way. The research follows what happens to my decisions and actions as an improviser in each case.

The work is practice-based and draws on rehearsal and performance diaries, video documentation and a set of case studies, each with a different dramaturgical framework.

Concepts

Focal points: techniques for configuring the relations between sound, movement and the space around the performer, combining musical improvisation exercises, Viola Spolin's Point of Concentration and a practice of focussed noticing.

Live electronics as a disembodied character: electronics systems built on coherent timbral corpora that act as a presence of their own on stage.
